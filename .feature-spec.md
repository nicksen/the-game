# Make the dummy stand up properly

## Problem

The dummy can't stand. It has behaved like this since the first commit; it isn't a regression.

`steerDummy()` in `src/dummy.ts` only pulls the pelvis, neck and head upwards. Nothing controls the legs, so a
straight leg buckles like a balanced stick: the knees bend sideways, the feet slide apart into the splits, and
when the body pulls itself together the legs cross (left foot to the right of the pelvis). Players see legs that
look flipped and a dummy that sits or kneels instead of standing.

Measured with a deterministic headless harness (fixed clock and random seed, positions in px above the floor):

| Situation                   | Pelvis (standing pose is 80) | Knees | Feet relative to pelvis |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----- | ----------------------- |
| spawn, 0s                   | 80                           | 42    | −16 / +16               |
| spawn, 1s                   | 34                           | 10    | −64 / +64 (splits)      |
| spawn, 10s                  | 59                           | 20    | +19 / −19 (crossed)     |
| 8s after a knockdown        | 58                           | 19    | −41 / +41               |
| starting at full effort, 1s | 49                           | 10    | +19 / −19 (crossed)     |

Experiment: also pulling the feet under the hips (pelvis.x ∓ 16) and the knees onto the hip–foot line keeps it
up at spawn (pelvis about 68) and gets it up 5s after a knockdown, but the knees bow out (±27) and it falls
again: the impact of its own fall counts as a hit (`damage()` sets `lastHit`), which resets the stand effort.

## Goal

Once nothing is stopping it (not dragged, not dizzy, not recently hit), the dummy gets up and stays up:

- feet under the hips, left foot on the left
- knees close to straight, bending forward slightly rather than sideways
- stable once up, including through its idle animations (wave, stretch, tap, look, watch)
- unchanged everywhere else: ragdoll falls, hits, grabbing, zero-g rooms, coins

## Plan

### 1. A stand-up test (written first, failing today)

- `tests/stand.mjs`, run by `mise run test:stand` and in CI.
- The test needs the dummy's pose, but the shipped game has no globals and shouldn't get test-only code. So the
  test bundles its own entry, `tests/stand-entry.ts`: it imports the game (`src/main.ts`) and puts `B` and the
  stand state on `window`. The test serves `index.html` with that bundle instead of `build/game.js`.
- Same frozen clock and random seed as the replay, so it's deterministic.
- Checks, with positions measured above the floor:
  - **spawn**: within 1s the pelvis is at least 70 and stays there for the next 5s
  - **legs**: while standing, `lFoot.x < pelvis.x < rFoot.x` and each knee is within 12px of the hip–foot line
  - **knockdown**: after a strong push, once it's allowed to stand it's up (pelvis ≥ 70) within 4s and
    stays up for 5s with no input
  - **idle**: standing survives a full cycle of idle animations
  - **zero-g**: in the space room it still floats; nothing pulls it to a standing pose
- One test per behaviour in turn, as the controller grows.

### 2. Leg control in `steerDummy()`

While standing and a foot is on the floor:

- pull each foot sideways to its spot under the hip (`pelvis.x ∓ 16`), keeping its height
- pull each knee towards the hip–foot line, nudged a few px forward, so it bends forward rather than sideways
- keep the existing pelvis, neck and head pulls

Tune strengths against the test, not by eye.

### 3. Its own fall shouldn't stop it standing

Impacts from the dummy's own collision with the floor (`collideDummy()` → `damage(..., 'impact')`) still hurt
and pay coins, but shouldn't reset the stand effort the way a player's hit does. Only add this if the test shows
it's still needed after step 2.

### 4. Re-record the replay baseline

The dummy moves differently, so the replay baseline changes. You play it first; once you're happy, re-record
with `mise run test:update` in the same commit as the change.

### Decisions

- `pull()` now damps vertical momentum as well as sideways; upward nudges used to build up into launches. Zero-g
  drift is gentler as a result.
- The stretch idle is half strength: pulling the arms harder lifts the whole dummy, because the idle pulls are
  outside forces. Balancing them with a push on the neck, torso or whole body squashed the legs instead.
- Removed the knee-to-knee stick: it stopped the knees passing each other, so crossed legs stayed crossed.
- Leg control stays on up to 10px of tiptoe, and the pelvis aims for its height above the floor, not the feet.
- Elbows get a resting spot in the relaxed pose; pulling them towards the neck–hand line lifted the dummy.
- Getting up: the pelvis holds the spot where it started getting up (once a foot is on the floor), the effort
  ramps up in 10 frames instead of 50, and sideways swing is damped while standing. Hits stop it standing, so
  knockback is unchanged. The time it stays down after a hit is unchanged.
- The test reads the pose through a test-only entry (`tests/stand-entry.ts`); the shipped game has no test hooks.
