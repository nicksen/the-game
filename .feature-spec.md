# Let the dummy walk around while idling

Piece 1 of 4 of richer idle behaviour. Later pieces, each with its own spec: more animations, reacting to the
player, playing with the room (which builds on walking).

## Goal

When left alone, the dummy now and then strolls to a new spot in the room with a cartoon walk, then carries on
idling there. It stays a ragdoll the whole time: a hit mid-stride knocks it over like any other time.

## Design (approved in brainstorming)

Approach: move the spot the dummy holds while standing, and step the feet. Rejected: a keyframed walk with physics
off (fights the ragdoll, pops when switching), and a shuffle that only slides the standing spot.

### Behaviour

- `walk` joins the idle actions (`IDLE_ACTIONS` in `src/dummy.ts`) and is picked at random like the others, never
  in zero-g (like `tap`).
- It picks a target 150–350px away, on whichever side has room, keeping at least 120px from both walls. If neither
  side has room (narrow phone screens), it picks a different action instead.
- It strolls there at about 1.5px per frame, then the next idle action starts from the new spot. Unlike the other
  actions, a walk ends when it arrives, not on a timer.
- Now and then it says a line on the way, through the existing dialogue system (new `LINES.walk`, e.g. "Just
  stretching my legs...").

### How it moves

- The walk moves the standing anchor (`getUpX`, the spot the pelvis holds) towards the target.
- Steps take about 0.6s. The planted foot is held where it is; the swinging foot follows an arc from behind the hips
  to ahead of them, lifted about 12px at the top.
- While walking, these step targets replace the "foot under its hip" rule in `placeLeg()`. Knees stay on the
  hip–foot line as now.
- Arms swing opposite to the legs; the body bobs slightly with each step.

### Interruptions

Anything that stops idling (a hit, a grab, a zap, the airlock) ends the walk. Because the walk only moves the
anchor, getting up afterwards works exactly as now.

## Plan

Test-first, one check at a time, in `tests/stand.mjs` (walking is standing behaviour). The test entry
(`tests/stand-entry.ts`) also exposes the walk target, so the checks can compare against it.

1. **Picking a walk:** `walk` plays during the idle check, and every target is inside the room with 120px to spare.
2. **Getting there:** each walk covers at least 100px and ends within ±20px of its target; the dummy never comes
   closer than 100px to a wall.
3. **Stepping:** while walking, the feet alternate (one planted, one swinging), stay on their own sides, stay within
   15px of their step targets, and the planted foot doesn't slide (under 2px per frame).
4. **Upright:** while walking, the pelvis stays between 70 and 90px.
5. **Arms and bob:** arms swing opposite to the legs. Checked by playing it rather than by a test.
6. **Interruptions:** a hit mid-walk knocks it down, and it gets up in place.
7. **Zero-g:** it never walks in the space room.
8. **Narrow rooms:** at a phone-sized width it only walks when there's room.

Each step: a failing check, then the smallest change that passes it. Then you play it, and the replay baseline is
re-recorded in the same commit as the change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] it stays clear of the walls, never walks in zero-g, and skips walking when the room is too narrow
- [ ] a hit mid-walk knocks it over, and it gets up in place as now
- [ ] `mise run test:stand` covers the checks above; all existing checks still pass

### Decisions

- Steps are 15 frames (0.25s) at 1.2px a frame, not 0.6s: with these legs, longer steps sagged into a near-split
  and let the hips overtake the leading foot. Worked out from the step geometry (hips travel 18px a step).
- It faces us, so the walk is a side-step: the leading foot steps first and reaches 10px ahead of its hip, the
  trailing foot closes in under its own; the stepping foot eases sideways so it doesn't scrape.
- A walk ends on arrival once the current step has landed.
- The arm swing is sideways only; swinging a hand up lifted the whole dummy. The bob comes from the legs on its own.
- The stepping check tests what you see (feet lift, never both up, planted foot still, feet on their sides)
  rather than internal step targets.
- Found along the way, committed separately: getting up from lying on its side swayed 35px; the hips are now held
  in place while the upper body swings up. A walk in zero-g could never arrive, so walking needs a floor.
- The stand test runs each scenario in a fresh game, so they can't affect each other.
